MacNetScan 2
============

A network scanner for System 7 on the classic Macintosh. MacNetScan sweeps the
local network over Open Transport and shows every device it finds - address,
name, round-trip time and one LED per service - and listens to what the
network says on its own: AppleTalk, ARP, DHCP, multicast DNS, LLDP and more.
It looks the part: a platinum tool bar of bevelled buttons as in the Excel of
1995, a radar scope that turns while it scans, Excel's dotted grid in every
table. Version 2 is a rebuild of MacNetScan 1 with Symantec's Visual Architect
and the THINK Class Library; it was written, compiled and tested through
AppleBridge.


The six views
-------------
IP Network   Sweeps the own /24: a concurrent, asynchronous TCP connect to ten
             well-known ports per address (AppleBridge, HTTP, HTTPS, SMB,
             NetBIOS, AFP, SSH, IPP, FTP, Telnet). Names come from NetBIOS,
             multicast DNS or reverse DNS. Round-trip times are taken when the
             answer arrives, to a tenth of a millisecond. Click a host for the
             detail panel.
AppleTalk    This Mac's node and zone; every NBP entity in every zone with a
             type LED and its AEP echo time; click one for what is known about
             it (an AFP server's machine, AFP versions and login methods, the
             other services on that node, the volumes mounted from it). Below:
             the AppleTalk traffic Listen hears, per protocol and per node; the
             routers and zones from RTMP and ZIP; the mounted volumes.
Listen       Passive: sends nothing but a few multicast joins. Shows what
             reaches this Mac anyway - ARP, IP broadcasts and multicasts
             (mDNS), DHCP, NetBIOS, IPv6, LLDP, AppleTalk and AARP - as
             protocol meters, a table of stations (MAC, IP and AppleTalk
             address or LLDP name) and an event log: new stations, gratuitous
             ARP, DHCP, IP address conflicts, LLDP neighbours.
Tools        Ping (TCP connect to port 80), DNS lookup (both directions) and
             traceroute (ICMP).
Map          Who sends to whom, from what Listen hears: the busiest stations
             around an oval, the groups they send to (broadcast, mDNS, IPv6
             multicast, LLDP, AppleTalk) inside it. Click a node for its box.
Frames       The capture behind Listen as a packet list, the selected frame's
             protocol tree and its bytes in hex - the last 400 frames. Export
             writes them as a pcap file for Wireshark.

Scan starts what the current view does (the sweep, the NBP lookup, listening);
Stop ends it. Export saves what the view shows as tab-delimited text (in Frames
a pcap file), Copy puts it on the clipboard. Scan > Options sets the address
range, the timeout, the number of probes at once and the port list; they are
kept in "MacNetScan Prefs" in the Preferences folder.

The grid button beside Copy, or View > Grid Lines (Command-G), switches the
dotted grid. A click on the radar scope opens the About box.


Demo mode
---------
View > Demo Mode (Command-D) is for screenshots and videos you want to put
online: host, AppleTalk and LLDP names become made-up ones, zones and volumes
neutral ones, MAC addresses and IPv6 interface IDs invented (but the same
device keeps its made-up name everywhere), and the hex dump in Frames is
hidden. It changes only what is drawn - Export, Copy and the pcap file stay
real.


Requirements
------------
- System 7 with Open Transport 1.1 or later (e.g. 7.5.3 to 7.6.1).
- A 68K Macintosh (or a PowerPC one running 68K code), colour, 720 x 460 or
  more for the window; 1500 K of memory.
- Tested under Basilisk II with System 7.6.1.


Under Basilisk II
-----------------
The Listen view sees this Mac's own traffic, broadcasts, the multicast groups
it joins, and whatever the host's network card receives. MacNetScan never asks
the Ethernet driver for raw or promiscuous mode: under Basilisk II both
requests hang the emulated Mac - and they would add nothing there.


Authorized use only
-------------------
MacNetScan actively probes hosts (port connects, NBP lookups, ping,
traceroute). Use it only on networks you own or are explicitly allowed to scan.
Scanning other networks may break acceptable-use rules or the law and can set
off intrusion-detection systems. The author accepts no responsibility for
misuse.
